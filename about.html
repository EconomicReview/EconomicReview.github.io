What we publish

The Review publishes two kinds of work by W&M students. Research is the journal’s core: full-length empirical or theoretical papers, selected by double-blind review against semester deadlines. Perspectives are 600–1,200 word op-eds and policy briefs, reviewed by editors on a rolling basis — the fast lane for a well-argued idea. Economics is the center of gravity, but public policy, finance, economic history, and data science applied to economic questions all belong here.

How review works

Every research submission is anonymized before anyone reads it. Only the Editor-in-Chief can connect a manuscript to its author; referees never see names, and authors never learn who read their paper. Two referees read each submission independently — no reviewer handles more than two papers in a cycle, so each paper gets a careful read — and the editorial board makes the final decision on their recommendation.

Perspectives skip refereeing: the editors read each piece and respond directly, usually within a few weeks.

In both tracks, every author receives written feedback with the decision. That includes rejections. We think the feedback is worth as much as the publication line, and it’s a promise most journals don’t make.

Where published work lives

Accepted work is published on this site. For research, the version of record will also be deposited in W&M ScholarWorks, the university library’s permanent open-access repository — the same infrastructure that keeps journals like the James Blair Historical Review available across generations of student editors. A journal run by students turns its staff over every few years; the library does not.
