Every submission gets written feedback

Whatever the decision, you will receive substantive written comments on your work — from two referees for research, from the editors for Perspectives. A rejection from the Review still leaves you with a better paper. Most journals don’t do this; we consider it the point.

Preparing a research submission

Research manuscripts are reviewed blind, so the file you upload must not identify you:

  • Remove your name, acknowledgements, and anything that names your section, professor, or co-authors from the manuscript.
  • Strip the document metadata too — in Word, use File → Info → Inspect Document; exported PDFs inherit the author field from the source file.
  • Upload a separate title page with the title, all authors and emails, and your class year — only the Editor-in-Chief sees it.
  • Include an abstract of 150–250 words and your word count.
  • Add 3–5 keywords; JEL codes are welcome but optional.
  • If the paper uses data, be ready to state what the data are and whether you can share them and your code.

Preparing a Perspective

  • Lead with your thesis — the form asks for it in one sentence.
  • Keep it between 600 and 1,200 words.
  • Link your sources; claims need evidence even at op-ed length.

For every submission

  • The work must be your own, and not under consideration at another publication while we review it.
  • The form asks how, if at all, AI tools were used in the research and writing — answer candidly.
  • Accepted work is published under your name on this site, and research is deposited in W&M ScholarWorks, the library’s permanent open-access repository; the form asks for your consent.
  • Co-authors from other institutions are welcome; the submitting author must be a current W&M student.
